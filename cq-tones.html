The four tones

Category for category, CQ words match standard. The SHAPES don't. Values below are the realized contours; sources show live variation (tone 1 between 55 and 45 — treat that band as one target zone).

The ladder

mā má mǎ mà → 55 · 21 · 42 · 213stdCQ

The four-way ladder, both voices. CQ tone 2 is a LOW FALL (not a rise), tone 3 a HIGH FALL, tone 4 a LONG LOW DIP.

tóu — std rise 35 → CQ low fall 21stdCQ

The hardest habit to break: CQ '2nd tone' falls. Hear it in wútóu (at home), duìtóu (exactly!).

yào — std fall 51 → CQ long dip 213stdCQ

Std's sharp 4th tone becomes a drawn-out low dip in CQ.

The folk rule: tones 3–4 swap shapes

hǎo — std dip → CQ 42 fallstdCQ

'Good' — the shape-swap demo.

shì — std fall → CQ 213 dipstdCQ

'To be' — the mirror image.

sì shí — four-ten money rescuestdCQ

Four (sì, dip) vs ten (sí, low fall). In a taxi, tone is the ONLY difference — locals lean on it hard. (Also on Mistakes.)

CQ tone 3 (42) sounds like a std 4th tone; CQ tone 4 (213) sounds like a std 3rd tone. Sichuan teachers sum it up as 'tones 1-2 keep, tones 3-4 swap'. Your std hǎo lands as what a std ear hears as hào.

This is recognition FIRST: when a local's 'mà' (tone 4) sounds like a dipping 'mǎ' to you, they said the right word — your ears miscalibrated.

The entering tone — Chongqing's fingerprint

Old Chinese had a fifth, cut-off tone. Standard Beijing scattered those words irregularly across its four tones. Chongqing merged them ALL into tone 2 (low fall). A whole class of everyday words sits low, whatever tone your textbook says.

Minimal pairs — train here

bā (55) vs bá (21)stdCQ

scar vs eight.

qī (55) vs qí (21)stdCQ

wife vs seven.

shī (55) vs shí→sí (21)stdCQ

teacher vs lose — plus the flat tongue.

Std keeps both words tone 1; CQ drops the entering-tone member to the low fall. These pairs ARE the entering tone.

Generational note: speakers under ~40 are losing this (gazetteer-documented) — you'll hear both systems. The traditional low-tone pattern is what the in-law generation uses, and it stays your production target.

Sandhi & rhythm

What changes in connected speech

zàijiàn — second syllable flattens to 21stdCQ

Tone-4 + tone-4: the second syllable flattens to a low fall (main-city rule). Same in shìjiè (world), yàoshi (if).

rènzhēn — the rise drops before a high tonestdCQ

Tone 4 loses its rise before a tone-1 syllable (rèn-21-zhēn). Recognition skill — sources disagree on the full rule set.

wūtóu — the suffix never swallowsstdCQ

No neutral tone in the city core: the 'tou' of wūtóu/duìtóu keeps its full low fall where std swallows it.

No std yī/bù sandhi: both sit at their fixed entering-tone lows (yí gè, bú yào) in every position.

Recognition-only: kinship reduplication (pópo-type words) reportedly flips the second syllable high — SW-documented, CQ-specific values not openly published. Just know it exists.

Geography flag: the Wanzhou belt (northeast of the city) does tone-4 pairs the OPPOSITE way (second syllable high). You're learning the main-city rule.

Beyond the city: only the Jiangjin/Qijiang belt keeps a true fifth tone — background trivia, not your target.
