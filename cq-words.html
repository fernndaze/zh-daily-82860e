Old readings at the dinner table

A medieval sound layer survives in everyday words — the same words your HSK book says otherwise. These are comprehension-critical: whole sentences hide behind single shifted syllables. (Old-speaker layer; young locals are losing some of these — the dinner table of the in-law generation keeps them all.)

xiézi → hái zístdCQ

SHOES — sounds exactly like std 'child'. The most famous listening trap in the southwest.

jiē → gāistdCQ

Street — 'shàng gāi', going into town.

xián → hánstdCQ

Salty — dinner-table critical. 'Hǎo hán ō!'

pángxiè → páhāistdCQ

Crab — every crab stall says páhāi. (Chengdu-side says pánhǎi — recognition only.)

jiǎo → jióstdCQ

Foot/leg — lóujió 'downstairs', jiógǎn 'leg'.

ròu → rústdCQ

Meat — hotpot-table critical; the ou collapses to u and the r buzzes.

qù → qièstdCQ

To go — the most frequent verb of all, said qiè (locals spell it with the 'qiè' character).

standard: qù
xià yǔ → luóyǔstdCQ

To rain — the old word for 'fall' survives here, entering-tone low.

gǎnjí → gǎnchángstdCQ

Going to the periodic market — market days run on the town's number cycle.

shuìjiào (older: gào)stdCQ

To sleep — the old g- reading lives on in baby-talk 'shuì gàogào'.

chuīniú → cōng kézistdCQ

To shoot the bull — braggy chat (vs honest chat).

The address system

Getting address right is most of getting politeness right — the grammar of respect here is WHO you call them, not softening the 'you'.

niāngniāng (āyí's job)stdCQ

Auntie — ANY mom-aged woman; the daily address at markets. Said with an n- start (many locals keep it here) though the l-merger makes some speakers blur it — hear both. A few younger women hear it as aging them: read the room.

standard: āyí
yāor (one fused beat)stdCQ

Darling — parents to kids of ANY age, couples to each other. NOT for strangers (see Mistakes).

lǎohànrstdCQ

Dad — casual, affectionate, standard when talking about fathers.

standard: bàba

Daily life & identity

bàbàstdCQ

Open flat ground — 'bàbà chá' street tea, 'bàbà yàn' courtyard banquet. CQ life runs on the bàbà.

dǎ wǎngstdCQ

To people-watch / scope out the scene — classic harmless city pastime; the cheeky sense is standard banter.

yōwèistdCQ

Oh my! — the local yelp of surprise; longer when lulling a yāor to sleep.

standard: āiyō
xióngqǐstdCQ

'Rise up!' — the stadium battle cry used across the southwest; you WILL hear it at any game.

standard: jiāyóu
piě tuóstdCQ

Brisk, no-fuss — 'that was easy'.

nǎohuǒ → lǎohuǒstdCQ

Tough/annoying/brutal — about SITUATIONS, never 'angry at a person' (that's màohuǒ). Full story on Mistakes.

pā ěrduostdCQ

Henpecked husband — the classic in-law tease. Understand it when family jokes; use only on very close people.

Register: everything on this page is SAFE daily vocabulary except the tease row — affectionate mockery only between close people.
