The sentence-ending keyboard

Where std has ma/ba/a, CQ splits the job across a keyboard of endings whose MEANING lives partly in their tone. Learn the stance, not just the sound.

sā — 'obviously / you know'stdCQ

Certainty tag: urging, agreeing, self-defense. 'Nǐ gǎo kuàidiǎn sā!' Spelled several ways in dialect writing; sound is constant.

standard: ba
há — '..., right?'stdCQ

Confirm-tag: you believe the answer is yes. ('Wǒmen míngtiān bā diǎn zǒu há?')

standard: duì ba
hā vs hàstdCQ

Two more há-family tones: hā softens requests and goodbyes; hà is the reminder ('wait for me-HÀ').

suó — 'oh, REALLY?'stdCQ

Disbelief challenge: the news clashes with what you expected. Drawn out, mock-outraged. No std twin.

standard: ma
mài — neutral yes/no questionstdCQ

The std 'ma' slot: checking a guess. Tag use (obvious facts, gentle urging) stays plain 'ma'.

standard: ma
ò — warm falling tailstdCQ

Urging/reminding warmth: 'zǒu ò!' let's go, 'don't forget-Ò'. Slides DOWN — a long warm fall.

standard: ba
nán — softens wh-questionsstdCQ

The CQ face of std 'ne': 'what are you doing-NÁN?'

standard: ne
déma — 'as we both know'stdCQ

Pulls shared background in; doubles as strong agreement ('exactly!').

Recognition-only: 'gā' ('right?') is a Chengdu/Kunming tag, not city CQ — if a lesson teaches it as yours, that's the Chengdu flag again.

Aspect & negation

-dā = std 'le'stdCQ

Perfective AND sentence-final: 'chī dā fàn' (ate), 'don't forget-DĀ'. Urban speech also says a 'lao' sound in the same slots — hear both.

standard: chīle fàn
-dǎo / -qǐ = std 'zhe'stdCQ

Durative: 'zuò dǎo' sit (and stay), 'ná dǎo' hold it; 'qǐ' for ongoing states. 'Walking-walking, it rained-LE' stacks them all.

standard: zuòzhe
mò = std 'bié'stdCQ

Negative commands ONLY; 'bù yào' keeps just the 'don't want' meaning. 'Mò gǎowàng dā!' don't forget.

standard: bié wàngle
méidé = 'not have'stdCQ

Possession negator; the A-not-A question uses it too — next section.

standard: méiyǒu

Verb patterns std doesn't have

V yí hà, never V-VstdCQ

'A bit' is verb + yí hà: kàn yí hà (not kànkan), shì yí hà, děng yí hà. Biggest single production habit on this page.

standard: kànkan
V dé lái / V bù lái = know-howstdCQ

'He can drive' = he's a driver-class 'comes' to driving; question form asks it in one breath.

standard: huì kāi
V dé dǎo / V bù dǎo = potentialstdCQ

'Can/can't manage': kāi bù dǎo mén — the door won't open. On the phone: 'can you hear-DǍO me?'

standard: kāi bù liǎo mén
yào / bùdé / débùdéstdCQ

Asymmetric future: will / won't / will-they-or-not. 'Yàodé' = OK; 'yào bùdé' = no good.

standard: huì bu huì
yǒu méidé = A-not-A questionsstdCQ

'Do you have change?' builds the question on yǒu méidé.

standard: yǒu méiyǒu

The degree ladder

hēi + adjstdCQ

Pre-adjective 'very' — THE CQ marker (Chengdu keeps plain hěn). 'Hēi hǎochī!'

standard: hěn hǎochī
adj + dé hěnstdCQ

Post-adjective 'really': 'hǎochī dé hěn'. Combines freely with hēi.

adj + cǎn lestdCQ

Maximum degree, usually positive-fun: 'insanely delicious'. NOT 'miserable'.

standard: jí le
fēi là · jiāo kǔ · liū suānstdCQ

Frozen vivid prefixes: crazy-spicy (your hotpot word), burnt-bitter, sour-sharp.

standard: hěn là

Passive & discourse

zāo = 'bèi', with attitudestdCQ

Adverse passive: 'he got scolded-ZĀ by the boss'. Neutral passives are simply avoided. Standalone 'zāo le!' = uh-oh (pairs with ōhuō).

standard: bèi
dòushì = 'exactly'stdCQ

Agreement backchannel: 'duìtóu, dòushì!' Right, exactly!

drilled in your day04 sprint

jǐndǎo = 'keep (doing)'stdCQ

Duration with an impatient edge: 'why do you KEEP clutching the money-SĀ?'

standard: yìzhí

lǎgèstdCQ

Who/which — 'lǎgè zài qiāo mén?' who's knocking. Note the l- (merger applied).

standard: shéi

Here / there / where — the 'diǎn' demonstrative system. (Chengdu: zhè guō.)

standard: zhèlǐ
hǎo dō / hǎo jiǔstdCQ

How much / how long — question senses; std uses different words entirely. 'Hǎo dō qián?' at every stall.

standard: duōshao

To know — 'bù xiǎodé' = I don't know. You'll hear it within an hour of landing.

standard: zhīdào
yàodéstdCQ

OK/will do — doubled when enthusiastic: 'yàodé yàodé!'
