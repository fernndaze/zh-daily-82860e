Production mistakes — wrong vs. right

The wrong button plays what the mistake sounds like; the right button plays the fix. Most of these are register errors, not pronunciation errors — the words are real, the audience is wrong.

✗ nǐ ge guāwázi! → ✓ nǐ jiā wár hǎo guāi òwrongright

Calling strangers 'silly-child' — documented as the FIRST dialect word outsiders learn and throw at strangers; between strangers it's an insult.

locals hear: an insult — the affectionate reading exists only inside family/closeness fix: compliment the kid: 'your little one is so cute-Ò'
register: TEASE tier is for close people only
✗ lǎozi bùdé gàn! → ✓ ér huō, wǒ bú gànwrongright

Self-aggrandizing rough talk parroted from viral clips — from a stranger it claims seniority or picks a fight.

locals hear: claiming to be their father / starting a fight — the rough-warmth register is invisible from outside fix: ér huō ('honest!') carries friendly bluntness safely
register: coarse fight-words, never initiate
✗ nín hǎo → ✓ lǎoshī, nǐ hǎowrongright

Textbook-Beijing politeness backfires: the south has no 'nín' — it reads stiff, distant, or sarcastic.

locals hear: drawing a line / mocking formality — southern elders are uncomfortable being 'nín'd fix: politeness rides the ADDRESS word: lǎoshī for anyone
politeness system differs, not missing
✗ shīfu → ✓ lǎoshīwrongright

'Shīfu' reads as tradesman-address; the city's universal honorific is lǎoshī — shop staff call every customer that (~70% usage across central districts).

locals hear: slightly off — like addressing a waiter as 'garage mechanic' fix: lǎoshī plus plain nǐ, plus the CQ question word
the safe universal address is lǎoshī; it 'basically can't be wrong' in CQ
✗ yāor, lái ma! (to a stranger) → ✓ měinǚ, qǐngwèn yíxiàwrongright

Using 'darling' on an unknown young woman — that's flirting or claiming closeness; couples and parents own that word.

locals hear: flirting / claiming a relationship that isn't there fix: měinǚ (young women), niāngniāng (older women), lǎoshī (everyone)
endearment words are kinship-licensed
✗ nǐ shuō mò lí? → ✓ nǐ shuō sázi?wrongright

Learning a district variant ('mò lí' = what, from the northeast belt) — in the city it marks you rural, and it near-homophones 'touch you'.

locals hear: rural marker + accidental 'touch you' mishearing (a documented incident) fix: sázi is the city form
dialect-within-dialect: variants mark geography
Register tiers: SAFE — say freely, including with in-laws. TEASE — affectionate mockery between CLOSE people only. VULGAR street retorts — never taught here; you'll hear them anyway.

Listening traps — what you'll mishear

These aren't your errors — they're traps the accent sets for std-trained ears. Same word, both voices; the difference between the buttons is the trap.

zàoniè — 'pitiful', NOT 'sinful'stdCQ

Locals saying 'poor thing' gets parsed by your std dictionary as moral accusation; your 'evil-doing' production gets heard as 'pitiful'. Pure valence mismatch.

háizi — 'shoes', not 'child'stdCQ

'My háizi fell off' = my SHOE. The documented lost-child panic joke of every Sichuanese traveler. Real 'child' is wár/yāor.

sì shí — four vs tenstdCQ

The taxi fare trap: both flatten to s-, only tone separates them (four = dip, ten = low fall). When unclear, locals repeat or switch to Putonghua — you can too.

niúnǎi — don't expect the contraststdCQ

Your name's N/L distinction doesn't survive; anchor with reference words ('Níng like Ningxià') instead of correcting locals. The error is EXPECTING the contrast, not the merger.

nǎohuǒ — 'brutal', not 'furious'stdCQ

The driver describing traffic isn't angry at you — that's the situation word. Anger at a person is màohuǒ.

sázi? méidé. — tone frame is the accentstdCQ

Viral 'convert your std tones' tables are documented as wrong. The CQ frame (plus entering-tone lows) is what you're training here — this whole guide is the fix.

Chongqing is not Chengdu (identity edition)

bāshì dé hěn! (CQ) vs the Chengdu drawlstdCQ

Assembled-from-internet 'Sichuanese' (drawled endings, 'zǎgè', 'mòdé', the an-twang) reads Chengdu — locals will tease it as such. City forms: clipped endings, lānggè, méidé, ér huō, lóujió.

Hear, don't say

Words to recognize but never add to your production:

māyo — the mild oath. Passive only; reads coarser in some Chongqing mouths (quorum ruling: not taught for production).

zǎgè — Chengdu's 'how'. You'll hear it in Chengdu-heavy media; your form is lānggè.
