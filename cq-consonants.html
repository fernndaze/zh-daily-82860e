Consonants: flat tongues and the ng hum

Three mergers that reshape every sentence — and one stereotype the city doesn't actually have.

n becomes l — everywhere

City speech has no phonemic n. Every std n- word comes out l-. This is complete — including before the i/ü sounds where some Sichuan dialects keep a nasal. That detail is your Chengdu detector: Chengdu keeps it (nǐ ≠ lǐ there), Chongqing doesn't.

The merger, live

nǎohuǒ → lǎohuǒstdCQ

Tough/annoying — the flagship demo. (Meaning trap too: not 'angry' — see Mistakes.)

nǐ → lǐstdCQ

You — identical to 'reason' (lǐ) in CQ.

niú → liústdCQ

Cow / awesome. Never std niú, never Chengdu's nasal version.

niúnǎi → liú lǎistdCQ

Milk — double merger. The 'liú-nǎi-niú' tongue-twister is exactly what locals can't keep apart.

nián → lián · nǚ → lǚ · niáng → liángstdCQ

Year, female, aunt-class — the high-vowel words where Chengdu differs. In CQ they all land on l.

Practical: when spelling your name, anchor it with a reference word ('Níng like Ningxia') — locals physically can't hold n/l apart, and correcting their 'mispronunciation' is the real foreigner mistake (see Mistakes).

Flat tongues: no zh/ch/sh — and r buzzes

shì → sìstdCQ

'To be' = 'four'. The cleanest single demo.

shān → sānstdCQ

Mountain = three.

zhǎo → zǎostdCQ

To look for = early.

lǎoshī → lǎosēistdCQ

Teacher — the universal address word, with its drawn-out final.

rén → zén (buzz like 'z' in zoo)stdCQ

The std r- becomes a buzzing voiced z — flat tongue, vocal cords on.

rènshi → zèn sìstdCQ

To know (someone) — double buzz.

rè → zêstdCQ

Hot — buzz plus the e-vowel shift (see Vowels).

The ng hum (and a faint v)

Vowel-initial words take a velar nasal onset. It's safe, strongly local, and fading only partially in young speakers (they drop the hum but keep CQ finals).

wǒ → ngǒstdCQ

I/me — the most frequent word in the language, humming.

ài → ngàistdCQ

To love — open syllable, maximally audible hum.

yìngshì → ngèn sìstdCQ

Really/truly — the signature intensifier (drilled in your sprint).

ānjìng → ngān jìnstdCQ

Quiet — double shift: ng hum AND ing→in.

wǔ kuài → vǔ kuàistdCQ

Five kuai — old-school speakers add a true v- onset to w-words (fading fast; hear it, no need to force it).

The negative ruler: f/h does NOT merge

fēijī — stays fēijīstdCQ

Airplane. City keeps f- and hu- fully distinct.

huí — stays huístdCQ

To return. City keeps it — 'féi' would mark the far districts.

Internet 'Sichuanese' loves the f/h merger jokes ('airplane' huījī). In the CITY that merger doesn't exist — it marks the peripheral districts (Hechuan/Tongnan/Rongchang and the Wuxi-Fengjie belt). Saying huījī in Yuzhong is a small comedy error in the other direction: over-applying the stereotype.

This guide flags these 'negative rulers' — places where the difference between city speech and the stereotype IS the lesson.

Anti-overgeneralization #1: f/h merging is NOT Chongqing city speech. Don't perform it.
